# Milestone 10: what the slice still lacked

Written 2026-10-23, after six weeks idle. The bar is §44 of `BROWSER_AAA_ENGINE_PROJECT_KICKOFF.md` (one complete mission of the Task Unit sequel: three objectives, 8 to 12 enemies with routes and level alert states, extraction, 5 to 10 minutes, showing movement, combat, enemy AI, interaction, environment, dynamic lighting, particles, animation, post and audio) and `docs/design/mission-shape.md` (Insert → three objectives → Extract, a checkpoint at each, Reach / Plant / Eliminate as level data, the stealth-to-combat alert model).

## Where it stood on 2026-09-07

The street ran: reach the square, set the charge on the depot wall, return to the insert. Eight patrolling riflemen on a baked navmesh, the per-enemy awareness ladder, the Quiet → Alerted → Lockdown sector model with reinforcements from authored ingress points, hitscan gunplay with gore and ragdolls, 113 generated audio takes, checkpoint reloads. 415 tests, seven gameplay probes.

| 9 | **Perf baseline red.** Every entry read high on `cpuMs` against a baseline recorded on a quiet machine, and the mission's row predated the draw-call work (1053 draws then, 721 since). | perf suite | **closed**: on a quiet GPU on 2026-10-23 the unchanged baseline read green across all nine entries, so September's red was contention. | 10 | **One lane per objective.** The street is a single 110 m corridor with sidewalks; the shape asks for two or three approaches per objective and verticality through mantle zones and ladders. | mission shape (level structure) | **closed**: three lanes, all authored in `street.py` and baked into the navmesh. *The alley*: the west row now stops at x = −20 and a 3.6 m service lane runs behind it for 72 m (z −70 to +2), walled by a row of back blocks 9 to 19 m tall, dressed dark (sparse windows, steel doors, pipes, AC units, fire-escape landings, cables, steam, trash) with five dumpsters, two knee walls, crates and three small light pools. Three passages join it to the street: by the square (4 m wide), at the broker's beat (2.4 m) and at the depot end (3 m). A fourth gap is closed by a 1.1 m knee wall the operator can vault and hostiles cannot, with a brazier burning in its dead end. Rifleman 5 moved off the square to watch the alley (up the lane, then halfway down the broker's passage), and the alerted wave's `side_west` ingress now comes in from it. *The overwatch*: a fire-escape stair up the east facade from the square's corner to a catwalk at 4.5 m that runs 27 m past the square and over the broker's beat; the stair is a tilted box collider under rendered treads, a knee-high apron stops a walk-off without being cover, and the line from its far end to the broker is 19 m long and 4.8 m down. *Mantle*: `spark.type=mantle` zones (footprint and top) on 37 low obstacles (single crates, barriers, road barriers, dumpsters, knee walls); Space near and facing one is a half-second scripted vault over it or onto it (`actors/mantle.ts`, `Operator`), on the fixed step, and Space anywhere else is still a jump. Every other gap between buildings, both sides, is now walled: they had opened onto the empty ground behind the street and the navmesh walked out through them. The visibility meter learned the moon's shadow (one ray, `AWARENESS.moonShare`), without which the alley was dark only to the eye: the walk down the lane now averages 0.10 against 0.165 on open road and about 0.48 on the west sidewalk. Not done: the hostiles do not mantle. The pipeline's navmesh bake passes no off-mesh links, and `Enemy` steers horizontally on a controller with a 0.4 m step, so a link over a crate would walk him into it; the lanes are built so nothing needs one (every knee wall leaves a gap at one end), and the vault gap is the operator's alone on purpose. Probes `the-alley-is-a-way-through`, `the-overwatch-sees-the-broker` and `mantle-vaults-a-crate`. |
| 11 | **Length unproven.** Nobody has timed a real playthrough against the 5 to 10 minute target. | §44 | open, needs Greg. The scripted run (no stealth, no deaths, rounds switched off) is the floor; a real run is a playtest. |
| 12 | **Nobody ever lost contact** (found while building #3). Once a hostile went alert his awareness pinned at 1 and the sight check re-ran `becomeAlert` every tick, which refreshed the contact clock, so Alert → Searching → Unaware never happened while the operator lived and the sector could never settle back down in real play. Probes only ever broke contact by killing the operator. | mission shape (alert model) | **closed**: alert is entered on the transition only, and losing contact drops awareness below the alert line. `charge-is-defended` asserts the descent (the workers lose an operator who has gone, then go to the charge). |
| 13 | **The objective marker never hid or took its radius** (found in the captures). `RenderSync` writes visibility and scale from the entity every frame, so the runner's `marker.visible` and `marker.scale` were overwritten: every ring was 1 m and the ring stayed up behind the end of the mission. | bug | **closed**: both go through the `Renderable` and `Transform` components; a unit test holds it. |
| 14 | **The broker fought like a rifleman.** A named target held his ground and traded fire exactly as his guards did. | mission shape (Eliminate) | **closed**: alerted, he wants cover far from the operator and runs for it, peeks half as often, fights where he stands only when cornered, and his shout carries twice as far. Probe `the-broker-keeps-his-distance`. |
| 15 | **Nothing proved the sector calms down in play.** The director's settle rule had unit tests; no probe showed a real street going back to quiet once the operator is gone, which until #12 it never could. | mission shape (alert model) | **closed**: probe `the-street-calms-down` (alerted to quiet, the wave stood down). |
| 16 | **The WebGL2 tier took 64 s to boot and froze for 18–23 s at a time.** RenderSync re-enabled every light the budget had culled, so the first frame compiled every lit material with 22 lights; with that fixed, every change in the selected eight recompiled them all again. The level's lights were also never registered with the lighting system, so the neon fog cones never rendered. | §49 (the compat tier boots and plays) | **closed** in the engine: the budget culls by layer, and the unrolled tier draws a fixed pool of proxies that copy the most important lights each frame. Level lights register through `LevelLoaderOptions.lighting`. The scorch decal is warmed at load. Boot is 7.6 s, and the blast and gunfire run at 13–16 ms on both backends. |
| 17 | **A 2–3 s freeze just after the mission becomes ready** (found while re-baselining perf). It happened on HEAD as well as on the second lanes, and on an idle GPU as well as a loaded one: one long animation frame with no long task in it, so the time went to the GPU side, not to script. A player would see it as a hitch at the start of the mission, and it made `pnpm perf`'s mission reading bimodal (`tools/benchmarks/README.md`). | §44 (no hitches) | **closed** in the engine. A Chrome trace with the GPU process in it found 30 compute pipelines, each a 70–200 ms DXC compile, on the thread that presents frames. Those were the particle emitters' update kernels: first dispatched in the step after the warm-up, and every one its own shader, because three names storage buffers and uniforms after node ids. The kernels' nodes are now named (62 kernels, 10 shaders), and the warm-up dispatches the update kernels as no-ops and waits for them. The longest frame after ready went from 1,989–2,098 ms (idle GPU) and 2,929–2,941 ms (with a second mission rendering) to under 100 ms in every run. The compiles now finish behind the loading screen, about 1 s longer there, and smooth play still starts 0.7–1.5 s sooner. The probe `the-opening-frames-compile-nothing` holds it down. |
